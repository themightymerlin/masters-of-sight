Director

Hayao Miyazaki

Animated / Mythological Japanese 1941

Hayao Miyazaki is the greatest animator who ever lived and arguably one of cinema's supreme visionaries regardless of medium. His films are hand-drawn worlds of extraordinary detail, populated by characters who earn their complexity through behaviour rather than exposition. Flight, ecology, pacifism, the moral ambiguity of progress, vast themes carried by images so beautiful they bypass argument and go straight for something older. He makes children's films that contain more truth than most art cinema.

Signature Traits

  • Hand-drawn animation treated as a moral commitment, not nostalgia.
  • Female protagonists who act, choose, and fail without male rescue.
  • Environmental grief embedded in every frame involving nature.
  • Ambiguous morality. No pure villains, no easy heroes.
  • The interval, lingering on a landscape, a meal, a breath, as cinematic value.
