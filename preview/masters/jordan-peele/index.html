Director

Jordan Peele

Horror / Social American 1979

Jordan Peele arrived in film already fluent in genre and used that fluency to say things American cinema had been circling around for decades. His horror is sociological, the monster is always structural racism, performed identity, or the violence of being unseen. He shoots with the precision of a visual scholar who has absorbed everything from Kubrick to Spielberg and distilled it into something entirely his own. He made genre cinema feel urgent again.

Signature Traits

  • Horror as a vehicle for sociological precision, not just fright.
  • Visual references worn openly as argument rather than pastiche.
  • The slow reveal. Unease built through wrongness, not shock.
  • Black American experience as the lens, not the subject.
  • Comedy timing applied to dread, the two are structurally identical.

Major Works

  • Get Out (2017)

    A breakthrough in American cinema. Horror about liberal racism, shot with the precision of a documentary. Oscar for screenplay.

  • Us (2019)
