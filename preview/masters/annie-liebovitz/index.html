Photographer

Annie Leibovitz

Portrait American 1949

Annie Leibovitz is an American portrait photographer whose work has defined celebrity imagery for over five decades. Born in 1949, she rose to prominence in the 1970s as chief photographer for Rolling Stone, where she developed an intimate, narrative-driven approach to portraiture. Her work bridges intimacy and spectacle, often placing subjects inside carefully constructed worlds that feel both personal and epic.

Signature Traits

  • Cinematic staging. Elaborate sets. Narrative context.
  • Subjects positioned inside story rather than isolated from it.
  • Warm tonal palettes with painterly lighting.
  • Blending vulnerability with grandeur.
  • The environment becomes part of the psychology.

Major Works

  • Rolling Stone, 1980, John Lennon & Yoko Ono

    Lennon curled around Ono. Nude and clothed. Love and exposure in one frame.

  • Vanity Fair, 1991, Demi Moore

    Pregnancy framed as power, not concealment. It shifted how maternity could be portrayed in mainstream culture.

  • Portraits for Vogue

    Actors as characters. Royals as cinematic figures. Musicians as archetypes.

  • Wonderland (2021)

    350 images spanning five decades of fashion work, many previously unpublished. Foreword by Anna Wintour. The definitive account of how she moved through Vogue and what she made there.
