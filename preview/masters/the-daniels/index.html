The Daniels

Absurdist / Sincere American 2011

Swiss Army Man asked an audience to fall in love with a flatulent corpse and somehow succeeded. Everything Everywhere All at Once took that same conviction, that the sillier the premise the harder it has to work for its feelings, and used it to build one of the most structurally ambitious films in recent memory, then made it about a mother and daughter arguing in a laundromat. Two features in and they had already rewritten what audiences would accept as serious cinema, provided the seriousness was smuggled in through the absurd.

Signature Traits

  • Practical, tactile effects, a corpse that farts, fingers that become hot dogs, chosen over CG because texture makes absurdity believable.
  • Total emotional sincerity applied to premises built to sound like jokes.
  • Genre collision staged inside a single scene, sometimes inside a single cut.
  • Commercial and music video pacing carried straight into feature runtimes, never a slack frame.
  • Loneliness and reconciliation as the buried engine under every formal experiment.

Major Works

  • Swiss Army Man (2016)

    A dead body becomes transportation, water fountain, and best friend. The most sincere film ever made about a farting corpse.

  • Everything Everywhere All at Once (2022)

  • Turn Down for What (2013)
