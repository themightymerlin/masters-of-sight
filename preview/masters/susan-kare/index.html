Designer

Susan Kare

Interface / Iconographic American 1954

Susan Kare designed the visual language of the personal computer at a moment when no such language existed. Hired by Steve Jobs to create the interface for the original Macintosh, she invented the icons, typefaces, and visual conventions that billions of people now use without thinking about them. The trash can. The paint bucket. The hand cursor. The concept of desktop metaphor made literal. She did it on a pixel grid so constrained it resembled medieval tapestry, and the results were immediately, enduringly legible.

Signature Traits

  • Pixel-level precision as craft, not limitation.
  • Metaphor made visual. Complex functions reduced to recognisable objects.
  • Clarity as the only objective. Every icon must communicate in one glance.
  • The grid as creative constraint rather than creative restriction.
  • Warmth embedded in functional design. Making computers feel approachable.

Major Works

  • Macintosh desktop icons (1983)

    The trash can, the folder, the clipboard. The visual vocabulary of computing, invented from scratch on a 32x32 pixel grid.

  • Chicago and Cairo typefaces

    System fonts that defined the Macintosh visual identity. Designed for legibility at sizes where letters have only a handful of pixels each.

  • Playing cards for Macintosh

    The game that came with every Mac. Proof that utility and delight are not separate design objectives.
