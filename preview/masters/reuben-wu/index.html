Photographer

Reuben Wu

Aerial / Long Exposure British 1975

Reuben Wu lights landscapes the way a studio photographer lights a face. Using custom-rigged drones as airborne softboxes, he illuminates remote mountains, salt flats, and glaciers with a precision no sunset ever offers. The technique began by accident in 2014, when a stray truck's headlights cut across a time-lapse and revealed what controlled light could do to raw terrain. Every image is captured in a single exposure, with no compositing, so what looks impossible was, in fact, actually there. His work has entered the permanent collections of the Guggenheim, the Met, and MoMA.

Signature Traits

  • Treats the entire landscape as a single subject under studio lighting.
  • Uses drone flight paths themselves as compositional elements, not just light sources.
  • Shoots in single exposures with zero post-production compositing.
  • Favors civil twilight, the narrow window where ambient sky tone still holds.
  • Built his visual language from an accidental encounter, then engineered the repeatable craft around it.
