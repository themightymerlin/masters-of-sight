Dream Maker

Shigeru Miyamoto

Interactive / Playful Japanese 1952

Shigeru Miyamoto is arguably the most influential game designer who ever lived and one of the most significant creative forces in twentieth century entertainment full stop. Creator of Mario, Zelda, Donkey Kong, and Pikmin, he built entire vocabularies of interactive play from scratch and did it with a consistent philosophy: games should feel like toys, and toys should feel like discoveries. Every mechanic in a Miyamoto game exists because it is joyful to perform, not because it is technically impressive.

Signature Traits

  • Play as the primary value. Mechanics that feel good before they mean anything.
  • The garden as creative origin. His childhood exploration of rural Japan as design blueprint.
  • Iterative design: every element tested, discarded, rebuilt until the fun is indisputable.
  • Character design as pure emotion. Mario's silhouette readable at the lowest resolution.
  • Accessibility without condescension. Depth that reveals itself gradually.

Major Works

  • Donkey Kong (1981)

    The game that introduced Mario. Jump as a game verb, invented here.

  • Super Mario Bros. (1985)

    The template for platform game design. Every subsequent platformer has been in dialogue with this one.

  • The Legend of Zelda (1986)

    Open-world exploration with a sense of genuine mystery. The direct ancestor of every open-world game ever made.
