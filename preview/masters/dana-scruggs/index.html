Photographer

Dana Scruggs

Portrait / Documentary American 1985

Dana Scruggs is one of the most vital portrait photographers working today, known for images that center Black identity with extraordinary dignity and power. Self-taught and shooting primarily on film, she became the first Black woman to shoot a solo cover for Rolling Stone. Her work refuses the voyeuristic gaze that has historically framed Black subjects in photography, replacing it with sovereignty, intimacy, and unflinching presence.

Signature Traits

  • Film grain as a deliberate emotional texture.
  • Subjects photographed as authors of their own narrative.
  • Natural environments used as psychological mirrors.
  • Restraint in editing that trusts the image completely.
  • Political weight carried without polemic.

Major Works

  • Rolling Stone covers

    Historic first. A Black woman shooting solo covers for the magazine. LeBron James, Chadwick Boseman, among others.

  • The New Black Vanguard (2019)

    Featured in Aperture's landmark anthology curated by critic Antwaun Sargent. One of fifteen photographer portfolios, placing Scruggs alongside the most consequential Black image-makers working in fashion and art today. A curatorial verdict on where the medium is going and who's leading it there.

  • BEYOND (2022)

    A collaboration with Nike exploring Black athletes as mythological figures.

  • The New Black Vanguard: Photography Between Art and Fashion (Featured)

    Aperture's landmark anthology curated by critic Antwaun Sargent. Scruggs is one of fifteen photographers with a dedicated portfolio, alongside Tyler Mitchell, Nadine Ijewere, and Campbell Addy. The Guardian called them 'spearheading a new aesthetic in fashion and art.' The curatorial placement says everything about where she stands.
