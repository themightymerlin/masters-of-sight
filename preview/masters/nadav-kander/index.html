Photographer

Nadav Kander

Landscape / Portrait British-Israeli 1961

Nadav Kander makes stillness feel heavy. His landscapes and portraits share an unusual quality, the sense that something irreversible has just happened or is about to. He photographs China's Yangtze River, Obama's cabinet, anonymous strangers, and treats all of them with the same quiet gravity. His work is about impermanence. Everything in it is already leaving.

Signature Traits

  • Muted palettes that amplify rather than diminish emotion.
  • Subjects made small against vast, indifferent space.
  • Time embedded in the image. Not the moment, but the era.
  • Restraint as an act of respect.
  • Silence used the way a composer uses it.

Major Works

  • Yangtze. The Long River (2006)

    Prix Pictet winner. China's transformation compressed into a landscape elegy.

  • Obama's People (2009)

    New York Times Magazine. Power portrayed without theater, which is more powerful.

  • Dust (2014)

    Former Soviet nuclear testing sites. Beauty and contamination, indistinguishable.

  • Nadav Kander: The Meeting (Monograph)

    Portraits and landscapes gathered into a single argument about impermanence. David Lynch wrote the foreword. That tells you something about the frequency this work operates on.
