About Masters Of Sight

Masters Of Sight is a curated sanctuary for the visually obsessed. A place where craft is worshipped, where every frame is a deliberate act, and where the boundary between art and entertainment ceases to exist. This is not a portfolio. This is a resource for those who demand better.

Why This Was Created

I built this because the world is drowning in content and forgetting what vision actually looks like. Consider this a reminder.

My name is Benny Jackson. I built this on my own time, with my own taste, because no one was going to build it for me. That's kind of the whole point.

For the ones who can't look away from great work. You know who you are.

Photographer

Meg Loeks

Storytelling / Nostalgia American 19XX

Meg Loeks builds mid-century domestic life into tableaux, a woman in a vintage dress half hidden by a clothesline of mustard aprons, or mid-recipe beside a mint stand mixer while a white dog works on the roast. She works from Michigan's Upper Peninsula, where she also photographs her children outdoors, and she started on film as a teenager in a high school darkroom before coming back, mostly self-taught, once her kids arrived. Her light comes from the Dutch Masters: low, directional, and rich in tone, often a continuous light parked in a window behind white translucent curtains. Mustard, rust, and mint sit against ink-dark cabinets and teal walls, so a kitchen reads as a stage set and a chore reads as a scene. She has scaled back client work to shoot mostly family and friends, so the nostalgia is homemade and the chaos is staged on purpose.

Signature Traits

  • Mid-century domestic sets built as stage dressing, a clothesline of aprons, an ironing board, a hair dryer cap, a mint stand mixer.
  • A lone woman in vintage dress at the center, often turned away, face hidden by laundry or folded over a task.
  • A palette of mustard, rust, coral, and mint green against dark teal and ink-black backdrops.
  • Low, directional window light softened by white translucent curtains, with tones borrowed from Dutch Masters painting.
  • Household chores staged as slapstick, with children and a dog wandering through the frame.

Major Works

  • Pressing Matters

    A portrait series whose digitally enhanced image Steamrolled placed second in its category in 2026 and was also a finalist for illustrative portrait.

  • The Pecking Order

    A self-portrait that took first place in its category in 2025 and went on to a jury top five selection.

  • The Order of Things

    A conceptual series that drew honorable mentions in 2026 from two separate international competitions.

  • The Dishes Won't Do Themselves

    A 2025 jury top five selection, one of two of her images to make that cut in the same year.
