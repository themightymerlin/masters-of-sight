About Masters Of Sight

Masters Of Sight is a curated sanctuary for the visually obsessed. A place where craft is worshipped, where every frame is a deliberate act, and where the boundary between art and entertainment ceases to exist. This is not a portfolio. This is a resource for those who demand better.

Why This Was Created

I built this because the world is drowning in content and forgetting what vision actually looks like. Consider this a reminder.

My name is Benny Jackson. I built this on my own time, with my own taste, because no one was going to build it for me. That's kind of the whole point.

For the ones who can't look away from great work. You know who you are.

Cinematographer

Robert Elswit

Gritty / Journalistic American 1950

Robert Elswit shoots like a journalist who studied painting. His frames have the immediacy of someone who earned access to the room and the compositional intelligence of someone who knew exactly what to do once inside it. A decades-long collaborator with Paul Thomas Anderson, he built the visual language of Boogie Nights, Magnolia, and There Will Be Blood, three films that look nothing alike and all look exactly like him. His work has a specific texture, grain, handheld urgency, practical sources pushed past comfort, that feels lived in rather than designed. Nightcrawler alone would be enough, that sickly Los Angeles amber, the city as a predator's hunting ground, lit to make everything look simultaneously beautiful and wrong.

Signature Traits

  • Handheld work that feels motivated rather than restless, the camera moves because the scene demands it.
  • Practical and available light sources used as emotional registers, not just illumination.
  • A grain and texture in the image that feels earned rather than applied in post.
  • Los Angeles photographed as a psychological environment, the city's light weaponized as mood.
  • Long collaborations as creative deepening, thirty years with Paul Thomas Anderson producing a body of work with genuine cumulative weight.

Major Works

  • There Will Be Blood (2007)

    Oil derricks, desert heat, and Daniel Plainview's escalating madness photographed in wide anamorphic frames that make greed look like a landscape. Won the Oscar. Earned it completely.

    ▶ Watch

  • Nightcrawler (2014)

    Los Angeles at night rendered in a sickly amber that makes the city feel like it is consuming itself. Jake Gyllenhaal as a predator and Elswit's camera as his accomplice.

    ▶ Watch

  • Boogie Nights (1997)

    The San Fernando Valley in the 1970s shot with a warmth that curdles slowly into something darker. The steadicam sequences are studied in every film school worth attending.
