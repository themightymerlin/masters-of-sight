About Masters Of Sight

Masters Of Sight is a curated sanctuary for the visually obsessed. A place where craft is worshipped, where every frame is a deliberate act, and where the boundary between art and entertainment ceases to exist. This is not a portfolio. This is a resource for those who demand better.

Why This Was Created

I built this because the world is drowning in content and forgetting what vision actually looks like. Consider this a reminder.

My name is Benny Jackson. I built this on my own time, with my own taste, because no one was going to build it for me. That's kind of the whole point.

For the ones who can't look away from great work. You know who you are.

Dream Maker

Fumito Ueda

Minimalist / Melancholic Japanese 1970

Fumito Ueda makes games the way Tarkovsky made films, slowly, deliberately, and with an absolute refusal to explain himself. His trilogy of works, Ico, Shadow of the Colossus, and The Last Guardian, form the most coherent artistic statement in the history of interactive media. He strips away everything, dialogue, exposition, UI, hand-holding, until only the essential remains. What's left is loneliness, wonder, and a bond between two creatures trying to survive a world that dwarfs them completely. No one working in any medium has made silence feel more inhabited.

Signature Traits

  • Radical reduction. Every unnecessary element stripped until only emotional truth remains.
  • Scale as a psychological instrument. Colossi, cathedrals, and creatures that make the player feel genuinely small.
  • Wordless storytelling. Narrative communicated entirely through environment, gesture, and music.
  • The bond as the game. Relationship between two beings as the entire dramatic engine.
  • Melancholy as a design philosophy. Beauty and grief treated as inseparable forces.

Major Works

  • Ico (2001)

    A boy and a girl escape a castle together. Hand-holding as a game mechanic. One of the most quietly devastating things ever made.

  • Shadow of the Colossus (2005)

    Sixteen bosses. No other enemies. A vast empty world and a question the game never answers: are you the hero or the villain? Widely regarded as the definitive argument for games as art.

    PS4/PS5

  • The Last Guardian (2016)

    A boy and a giant feathered creature navigate ruins together. Eleven years in development. Worth every one of them.
